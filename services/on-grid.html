On Grid Solar System in Kerala for Homes & Businesses

Generate solar electricity during the day while staying connected to the KSEB electricity grid.

An on grid solar system—also called a grid-connected or grid-tied solar system—uses rooftop solar panels to power your property first. Depending on the applicable metering arrangement, surplus generation can be exported to the grid, while electricity can be drawn from KSEB when your solar system is not producing enough power.

How It Works

How an On Grid Solar System Works

An on the grid solar power system works alongside your existing electricity connection rather than replacing it completely.

During daylight hours, rooftop solar panels convert sunlight into DC electricity.

The solar inverter converts this DC electricity into AC electricity that your home or business can use.

The electricity generated by solar is first used by your appliances and other electrical loads.

When solar generation exceeds your immediate requirement, surplus electricity can be exported through the applicable grid-metering arrangement.

When solar generation is lower than your electricity demand—or at night—the property draws the required electricity from the KSEB grid.

An on-grid system normally does not contain a battery.

That is one reason an on grid solar system in Kerala usually has a lower initial cost than an equivalent hybrid or off-grid system.

Suitability

Is an On Grid Solar System Right for Your Home?

An on grid solar system for home can be a practical option when your main objective is reducing grid electricity consumption rather than obtaining battery backup.

It is commonly suited to homes and properties that have:

  • A reliable KSEB electricity connection
  • Suitable rooftop space
  • Good sunlight exposure
  • Moderate to high electricity consumption
  • No requirement for battery backup during every power cut

On Grid Solar for Residential Properties

A typical on grid home solar system can supply electricity for appliances such as:

  • Air conditioners
  • Refrigerators
  • Washing machines
  • Water pumps
  • Fans and lighting
  • Televisions
  • Computers
  • Kitchen appliances
  • EV charging loads where appropriately designed

The recommended solar capacity should be based on your actual electricity consumption rather than choosing a system only by roof size.

On Grid Solar for Businesses

On-grid systems are also commonly used for:

  • Offices
  • Shops
  • Clinics
  • Hotels
  • Schools
  • Warehouses
  • Factories
  • Commercial buildings

Businesses with substantial daytime electricity demand can use a high proportion of solar generation directly.

When On Grid Solar May Not Be the Best Choice

An on-grid system may not be the best choice if:

  • You need electricity backup during frequent grid outages
  • Your property has no reliable grid connection
  • Your roof receives significant shading
  • You need independent electricity supply at night

In these situations, compare hybrid or off-grid solar systems.

Components

What Is Included in an On Grid Solar System?

A professional solar panels on grid system contains several components working together.

On Grid Solar Panels

Solar panels capture sunlight and generate DC electricity.

Panel selection depends on:

  • Available roof area
  • Module wattage
  • Efficiency
  • Technology
  • Manufacturer warranty
  • Performance warranty
  • Local service availability

Higher-wattage panels can help fit more system capacity into roof areas where space is limited.

Grid-Tied Solar Inverter

The inverter is one of the most important components in a solar energy on grid system.

It converts DC solar electricity into AC electricity and synchronises the system with the electricity grid.

Inverter selection should consider:

  • Solar-array capacity
  • Number of MPPT channels
  • Roof orientation
  • Monitoring functionality
  • Manufacturer warranty
  • Service support

Solar Mounting Structure

The mounting structure secures the panels to your roof.

The design varies according to:

  • Flat RCC roof
  • Sloped RCC roof
  • Tile roof
  • Metal-sheet roof
  • Elevated installation requirements

The structure should be selected according to the property's roof condition and local environmental exposure.

Bidirectional / Net Meter

A grid-connected installation requires suitable metering to record electricity flowing between the property and the grid.

The bidirectional meter records electricity imported from the grid and surplus solar electricity exported from the renewable-energy system.

Electrical Protection

A complete solar panel on grid installation should include appropriate:

  • DC protection
  • AC protection
  • Isolators
  • Surge protection
  • Earthing
  • Cables
  • Connectors
  • Distribution equipment

Solar Monitoring System

Compatible inverters generally provide system-generation and performance data.

Monitoring helps users track:

  • Daily solar production
  • Historical generation
  • Inverter status
  • Alerts
  • Performance trends

Net Metering

How KSEB Net Metering Works with On Grid Solar

In a grid-connected renewable-energy system, solar electricity generated at the consumer's premises is first used to meet the property's electricity requirement.

When solar generation exceeds immediate consumption, surplus electricity can be exported to the grid.

The bidirectional meter records:

Electricity imported from KSEB
and
Electricity exported from the solar system

The applicable energy accounting, billing and settlement are governed by current KSEB and KSERC rules.

What Happens at Night?

Solar panels do not generate electricity at night.

An on-grid property therefore draws electricity from KSEB when solar generation is unavailable.

What Happens During Cloudy Weather?

Solar panels can still generate electricity under diffuse sunlight, but output may be lower.

The grid automatically supplies the remaining electricity requirement.

What Happens During a Power Cut?

A standard on grid rooftop solar system normally shuts down during a grid outage.

This anti-islanding safety feature helps prevent the solar system from energising grid lines while utility personnel may be working on them.

If backup electricity during outages is important, consider a hybrid system with suitable battery storage.
