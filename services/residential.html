Suitability Assessment

Is Your Home Suitable for Residential Solar?

Residential rooftop solar panels work well for many Kerala homes, but the right system depends on more than simply having an empty roof. Before recommending a system, FGE Solar reviews your electricity consumption, available rooftop area, shading, roof condition and future energy requirements. Consider the following factors.

Your Electricity Consumption

Homes with higher electricity consumption generally have more opportunity to offset grid electricity with rooftop solar.

Instead of choosing residential solar panels purely according to your bill amount, review the actual electricity units consumed across several billing periods. This helps determine a more appropriate system capacity.

Available Rooftop Space

Your home needs sufficient usable, structurally suitable and relatively shadow-free roof space.

The exact area required depends on:
  • Panel dimensions
  • Panel wattage
  • Roof type
  • Panel layout
  • Maintenance access
  • Required spacing
  • Shading conditions

A site assessment determines how much usable solar capacity your roof can realistically accommodate.

Roof Orientation and Shading

Solar output depends on the amount of usable sunlight reaching the panels.

Our assessment considers:
  • Roof orientation
  • Surrounding buildings
  • Trees
  • Water tanks
  • Parapet walls
  • Seasonal shadows
  • Panel tilt and layout

A roof does not need to face one specific direction to qualify for solar. The objective is to develop a layout that maximises usable generation from the available space.

Residential Electricity Connection

Eligible residential consumers can explore rooftop-solar installation and applicable government-support schemes. For grid-connected systems, technical feasibility, metering and final connectivity remain subject to the applicable KSEB process.

Your Long-Term Electricity Requirements

Tell us if you expect your household electricity usage to increase. Future requirements may include:

  • Additional air conditioners
  • Electric vehicles
  • Heat pumps
  • Home office equipment
  • Larger water pumps
  • Additional appliances

Your residential solar system should account for realistic future needs without unnecessarily oversizing the installation.

Not sure what your home needs?

Our team can assess the property and recommend an appropriate residential solar capacity.

Equipment & Components

What Does a Residential Solar System Include?

A complete residential solar system consists of more than rooftop panels.

Residential Solar Panels

Solar PV modules convert sunlight into direct-current electricity.

Panel selection should consider:
  • Wattage
  • Efficiency
  • Technology
  • Temperature performance
  • Product warranty
  • Performance warranty
  • Manufacturer support

The highest-wattage panel is not automatically the best option for every roof.

Solar Inverter

The inverter converts electricity generated by the solar panels into AC electricity that can be used by household appliances.

The inverter should be correctly matched to the solar-array design and electrical connection.

Mounting Structure

The mounting system secures the panels to the roof.

Its design should take account of:
  • Roof material
  • Local wind exposure
  • Panel layout
  • Drainage
  • Access for maintenance

Electrical Protection

A professionally designed residential solar installation should include appropriate isolation, earthing, surge protection and other electrical safety components required for the installation.

Monitoring System

Compatible solar inverters can provide generation and system-performance information through monitoring interfaces or applications.

Monitoring makes it easier to identify unusual generation patterns or system alerts.

Optional Battery Storage

Battery storage can be added where backup electricity is important.

A standard grid-connected solar system normally shuts down during a grid outage for safety.

Customers requiring backup should discuss a suitable hybrid solar and battery system during the design stage.
