KSEB Solar Fixed Charge for Solar Homes: Option-1 vs Option-2 Explained

Last updated: 29 September 2026 | Reviewed by FGE Solar technical team · 29 September 2026 · 10 min read

If you have rooftop solar on a domestic connection, the KSEB solar fixed charge on your bill can now be worked out in one of two ways. You can stay on the normal domestic slabs, based on the total units your home uses, or you can pay a flat ₹47 per kW per month on your connected load. Which one costs less depends on your own load and usage.

This guide explains the KSEB fixed charge for solar prosumers in plain words, with worked examples for 3 kW, 5 kW and 8 kW homes and the steps to switch.

Quick answer

Domestic solar homes in Kerala have two fixed charge options. Option-1 uses the tariff order slabs on your total consumption and is the default. Option-2 is ₹47 per kW per month on your total connected load, till further orders. To move to Option-2, write to KSEB at least one month before the date you want it to start.

Key takeaways

  • KSERC allowed two options on 22 September 2025 (OP No. 43/2025).
  • Option-1 (default): slab-based fixed charge on total consumption under the Tariff Order dated 5 December 2024.
  • Option-2: ₹47 per kW per month multiplied by your connected load. It does not change with how much solar you generate.
  • Option-2 suits a small connected load with high consumption; a large connected load usually does better on Option-1.
  • APTEL dismissed the appeal against the order on 16 February 2026 (APL No. 384 of 2025). Both options remain.
  • The ₹47 rate is interim. KSERC has been directed to work out a lasting method.

What is the KSEB solar fixed charge?

A domestic bill in Kerala has an energy charge for the units you use and a fixed charge that recovers network costs separately. For a solar home, KSEB charged the fixed charge on total consumption, including solar units used directly at home. The KSEB solar fixed charge options you see today come from a challenge to that practice.

What KSERC decided in OP No. 43/2025

On 22 September 2025, KSERC decided OP No. 43/2025, filed by six domestic rooftop solar prosumers. It found no illegality in charging the fixed charge on total consumption under the net metering rules, but allowed two ways to pay: the existing consumption-based method (Option-1) or ₹47 per kW per month on connected load (Option-2), till further orders. The order also directed security deposit reviews under the Supply Code, a separate matter.

KSEB fixed charge for solar prosumers: the two options

Option How the charge is worked out What you must do
Option-1 Fixed charge from the LT-I domestic tariff slabs, based on total consumption in the billing period Nothing. This is the default.
Option-2 ₹47 per kW per month multiplied by your total connected load with KSEB Write to KSEB at least one month before the date you want to switch

Option-1 moves with your consumption slab. Option-2 stays the same every month unless your connected load on KSEB's records changes.

Option-1: fixed charge slabs from the domestic tariff

Under Option-1, the fixed charge follows the LT-I Domestic schedule in the Tariff Order dated 5 December 2024, which covers 1 April 2025 to 31 March 2027. For the full energy charge rates, see our guide to KSEB tariff slabs.

The higher your total consumption slab, the higher the fixed charge, even if most of those units came from your own solar panels.

What "total consumption" means for a solar home

Total consumption is all the electricity your home used in the billing period: grid imports plus solar units used directly during the day. It is not the net figure after exports that your KSEB net metering account settles.

So a solar home can sit in a higher slab than its import units alone suggest.

Option-2: the ₹47 per kW fixed charge

Under Option-2, the monthly fixed charge is ₹47 multiplied by your total connected load in kilowatts, as recorded with KSEB. It does not depend on your consumption, the weather or your exports. Note that it is based on connected load, not solar plant size, so a later load increase raises it.

Where the ₹47 figure comes from

The Commission arrived at ₹47 per kW per month from the average fixed charge recovery implied by the then-prevailing domestic tariff for 2025-26. It is an interim figure until a lasting method is set.

Option-1 vs Option-2: worked examples for 3 kW, 5 kW and 8 kW

This table compares the monthly Option-2 amount for three connected loads with the Option-1 slabs above (fixed charge line only).

Connected load Option-2 per month Single-phase: Option-2 is lower when monthly consumption is Three-phase: Option-2 is lower when monthly consumption is
3 kW 3 x 47 = ₹141 201 units or more (Option-1 is ₹160 at 201 to 250 units) 51 units or more (Option-1 is ₹175 at 51 to 100 units)
5 kW 5 x 47 = ₹235 301 units or more (Option-1 is ₹240 in the 0 to 350 slab) 251 units or more (equal at ₹235 in the 201 to 250 slab)
8 kW 8 x 47 = ₹376 Never (the highest Option-1 slab is ₹310) Never (the highest Option-1 slab is ₹310)

3 kW connected load

Option-2 would be ₹141 a month. On single-phase supply, Option-1 is ₹140 or less up to 200 units, so switching gains nothing below that; from 201 units Option-2 is lower. On three-phase supply, where slabs start higher, Option-2 is lower from 51 units.

5 kW connected load

Option-2 would be ₹235 a month. On single-phase supply, Option-1 stays lower up to 300 units (₹220), and Option-2 wins only from 301 units. On three-phase supply, the two are equal at 201 to 250 units, and Option-2 is lower from 251 units.

8 kW connected load

Option-2 would be ₹376 a month, higher than every Option-1 slab including ₹310 for above 500 units. Option-1 stays lower whatever your consumption.

In short, a small connected load with high consumption favours Option-2, and a large connected load favours Option-1.

How to read the fixed charge on your KSEB bill

Your KSEB bill details already hold the numbers you need: the connected load (in kW or watts), usually printed near your consumer number, and the fixed charge line in the charges section.

When you check your Kerala electricity bill details, also note your supply type (single-phase or three-phase) and total units consumed. If a bill covers more than one month, divide the fixed charge by the number of months before comparing.

For a quick KSEB bill calculation, multiply your connected load by ₹47 and compare it with the monthly fixed charge on recent bills. Our KSEB bill calculator can help you estimate the energy charge side of the same bill. If the numbers on your electricity bill KSEB issued do not match what you expect, ask your section office before making any change.

How to switch to Option-2

  1. Find your connected load on your KSEB agreement or latest bill.
  2. Multiply it by ₹47 to get your Option-2 monthly fixed charge.
  3. Check the fixed charge on three or four recent bills, including a high-usage month.
  4. If Option-2 is lower in most months, write to KSEB asking to move to Option-2 under OP No. 43/2025.
  5. Submit it at least one month before the date you want the change to start.
  6. Keep a dated copy of your request, and confirm with your section office that the option change has been recorded.
  7. Check that your first bill after the start date shows the Option-2 amount.

What APTEL said in February 2026

The same prosumers appealed to the Appellate Tribunal for Electricity (APTEL). In APL No. 384 of 2025, decided on 16 February 2026, APTEL dismissed the appeal and left the interim Option-2 arrangement in place. It directed KSERC to finish a lasting method based on connected load or recorded maximum demand quickly. Until a new KSERC order replaces them, both options remain in force.

What the fixed charge option does not change

Your choice changes only the fixed charge method, not the rest of your bill:

  • Energy charge still follows the tariff order and your net billing.
  • Fuel surcharge (when applicable) and meter rent are unchanged.
  • Net meter export and banking still follow the renewable energy regulations.
  • Any security deposit refund or top-up follows the Supply Code review rules.

Frequently asked questions

What is the ₹47 per kW solar fixed charge in Kerala?

It is Option-2 under the KSERC order of 22 September 2025 in OP No. 43/2025. Domestic solar prosumers who choose it pay ₹47 per kW per month on their total connected load, till further orders, instead of the slab-based fixed charge.

Do I have to switch to the ₹47 per kW option?

No. Option-1 is the default. You can keep paying the domestic fixed charge based on total consumption under the Tariff Order dated 5 December 2024. The Commission approved both options, and the order does not force anyone onto Option-2.

Which is cheaper for a solar home, Option-1 or Option-2?

It depends on your connected load and usual monthly consumption. Multiply your connected load in kW by ₹47 and compare it with the fixed charge line on your recent bills. Small connected loads with high consumption tend to favour Option-2; large connected loads tend to favour Option-1.

How do I tell KSEB I want Option-2?

Send a written request to KSEB at least one month before the date you want the change to start. Keep a dated copy, and confirm with your section office that the option change has been recorded.

Did the court cancel the fixed charge for solar homes?

No. KSERC found no illegality in levying the fixed charge on total consumption for domestic net-metered prosumers. APTEL dismissed the appeal on 16 February 2026 in APL No. 384 of 2025, so both options remain.

Is ₹47 per kW a permanent rate?

No. Option-2 applies till further orders. APTEL directed KSERC to complete a lasting method based on connected load or recorded maximum demand quickly, so the rate may be replaced by a later order.
